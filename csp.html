Background · No signal-processing knowledge required

What is a complex
steerable pyramid?

A way to describe image patterns by where they occur, how large they are, and which direction they run.

A complex steerable pyramid, or CSP, transforms an image into several maps of local patterns. Steerable Lens changes values in these maps and reconstructs an edited image. The pyramid is an image representation; the classifier supplies the direction of the edit.

One image, several views of its structure

Think of examining a face at different levels of detail: fine edges, medium-sized contours, and broad variations. At each level, filters respond to patterns with different orientations. Each response map still records where the patterns occur in the image.

The original steerable-pyramid work describes this decomposition into scale and orientation bands. Simoncelli & Freeman, 1995.

What do the three words mean?

Pyramid
Successive levels describe coarser spatial patterns. In a downsampled implementation, coarser response maps also have fewer pixels. Scale is about spatial detail, not a semantic category.
Steerable
Responses at other orientations can be formed from a set of basis filters. “Steerable” refers to filter orientation; it does not mean that the pyramid itself knows how to turn a cat into a dog.
Complex
Each oriented coefficient has a real and an imaginary component, from a pair of filters with complementary phase responses. Together they can be expressed as an amplitude and a phase. “Complex” here is a mathematical term.

The pyrtools tutorial shows actual real and complex pyramid decompositions.

Amplitude and phase: strength and alignment

Amplitude describes the strength of a local filter response. Phase describes its position within a local oscillation. For a simple repeating stripe pattern, changing phase shifts the stripes, while changing amplitude alters their contrast.

complex coefficient = amplitude × exp(i × phase)

This stripe example is an intuition, not a promise about arbitrary images. Phase changes across overlapping bands can interact, and large or inconsistent edits can create distortions. Phase is also unstable where amplitude is near zero. Amplitude and phase are not separate “appearance” and “meaning” channels.

Quadrature filter pairs and their local energy interpretation are discussed in Simoncelli, Freeman, Adelson & Heeger, “Shiftable Multiscale Transforms”.

How Steerable Lens uses it

  1. Represent the source. Decompose the image into pyramid coefficients.
  2. Compute an edit direction. Differentiate the classifier’s target objective with respect to the editable coordinates.
  3. Change coefficients and reconstruct. CSP phase changes phase; Joint CSP changes phase and amplitude. The experiment determines which bands and residuals are editable and when the gradient is recomputed.
  4. Evaluate the image. Compare methods at the specified image distance, and separately measure classifier responses, smooth-reconstruction compatibility, and human ratings where available.

Unlike global Fourier components, CSP bands are localized in position as well as scale and orientation. That makes them useful coordinates for exploring changes to local image structure. It does not guarantee a recognizable target change.

Our main MNIST and CelebA experiments use Plenoptic’s complex pyramid. The exploratory natural-image sequences use a custom LocalPyramid implementation. These are different implementations; their settings should not be assumed interchangeable. See the model and protocol table.

Reading the project’s results

  • Scale 0 means finest in the displayed band probes. It is not a universal pixel size; map dimensions depend on the input and implementation.
  • Phase-only does not mean a pure warp. Smooth-warp compatibility is measured after editing, rather than guaranteed by the representation.
  • Human recognition is an empirical question. The two-rater pilot provides preliminary evidence about selected attribute edits. The pyramid alone does not establish perceptual improvement.
